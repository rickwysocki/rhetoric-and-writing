\begin{frame}[label={sec:org1f781f2}]{Reverse Outlining}
A process of reviewing and assessing the structure of a draft to identify areas that could be revised/refined.
\end{frame}
\begin{frame}[label={sec:org46e93c9}]{Reverse Outlining: Step 1}
Read through your existing draft. As you do leave a comment for \alert{each paragraph} that identifies the larger topic of the paragraph.
\end{frame}
\begin{frame}[label={sec:org58433c9}]{Reverse Outlining: Step 2}
Now that you've read through the essay once, take a moment to write an answer to the following question: "What is the larger point or perspective I want to offer about my genre?" You can include your answer on a separate document or at the top of the draft.
\end{frame}
\begin{frame}[label={sec:org3129f77}]{Reverse Outlining: Step 3}
Read through your paper one more time. This time, leave a second comment for each paragraph that answers the following two questions:
\begin{itemize}
\item What information is included in this paragraph that supports the larger perspective I identified in the last step?
\item What additional information could be incldued in this paragraph to support that perspective?
\end{itemize}
\end{frame}
\begin{frame}[label={sec:orge4d4649}]{Reverse Outlining: Step 4}
Now, pick a single paragraph in your essay that you feel like could use some extra attention. For \alert{each sentence}, leave a comment explaining what value it offers to the paragraph. Examples might include:
\begin{itemize}
\item "This sentence introduces the topic."
\item "This sentence follows up on a quote to show how I'm thinking about it."
\end{itemize}
As you do this, take notes on how the paragraph could be improved.
\end{frame}
\begin{frame}[label={sec:org141091c}]{Reverse Outlining: Reflection}
Finally, take a few minutes to review the processes you've carried out and the notes you've taken. As you do so, try to identify some important areas of revision.
\end{frame}
